# Lessons Learned From a Year of AI Coaching at the Enterprise

## Description

Most enterprise AI rollouts are built on assumptions nobody has tested at the scale you're working at. Leaders commit budget, developer time, and political capital to interventions that sound right on paper — and then struggle to read the results when the dashboards all show green but something still feels off. After a year coaching dozens of teams through one of the largest Copilot rollouts in the wild, I don't have *the* answer. But I have a graveyard of things we tried that didn't work, a handful of surprises that actually moved behavior, and a clear picture of the experiments we're running right now to figure out what's next. You'll leave not with a finished playbook but with real experiments, real failures, and the honest questions still worth asking — which is more than most leaders in this room have.

## Organizer notes

For the past year I've been embedded as an enterprise AI coach inside one of the largest GitHub Copilot rollouts currently running — sitting in planning meetings, watching pull requests, tracking what changes developer behavior and what doesn't. This is genuinely a lessons-learned talk: I don't yet know THE answer, and I don't want to present it as if I do. The talk is honest about what we tried, whether it worked, and what we're still figuring out.

That framing is actually rarer and more valuable than another "here's the playbook" talk. For a leadership-heavy audience that has seen plenty of vendor-deck certainty, the credibility of "we're still running experiments" is the differentiator.

Target audience: engineering leaders, platform teams, and developer advocates running enterprise AI rollouts.

## Talk outline

### Opening: the assumption problem
- The setup: a year inside one of the largest Copilot rollouts in the wild
- What leaders think they're buying when they roll out Copilot — and why the feedback loop is broken from day one
- Frame the talk: this is what we tried, not what we solved

### Section 1: What we tried — and what happened
Each experiment gets: *what we tried → what we expected → what actually happened → what we think it means*

- **Training and enablement programs** — high effort, reached the already-enthusiastic; didn't move the others
- **Lunch-and-learns and office hours** — same audience every time; confirmed enthusiasm, didn't create it
- **Internal champions and evangelism** — works until the champion leaves or burns out; fragile by design
- **Seat-utilization dashboards as a success metric** — green metrics, behavior unchanged; measuring the wrong thing
- **Defaults baked into repo templates and CI** — the surprise: moved teams that never opted into anything else

### Section 2: What surprised us
- The developers we couldn't reach through any opt-in channel *did* change behavior when defaults changed around them
- The qualitative signal that was more predictive than any dashboard metric (and the question behind it)
- The well-intentioned move that felt like surveillance and created resentment instead of adoption
- What "success" looks like 6 months in vs. what it looks like at 30 days (they're different)

### Section 3: What we're still figuring out
- The experiments currently running and what we're watching for
- The questions I still can't answer confidently — and why I think they're worth stating openly
- What I'd change on day one if I were starting this engagement over

### Close
- What this might mean for your rollout — framed as hypotheses, not prescriptions
- The one thing I'm most confident about and the one thing I'm least confident about
- Q&A — oriented around the experiments attendees are running and the questions they can't answer either
