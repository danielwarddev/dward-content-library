## Conference Theme / Focus

> 🚀 **Agentic Coding is becoming the norm, are you ready to scale?**

If 2025 was the year AI coding agents gained adoption, 2026 is the year to make them work **reliably and at scale**. The conference focuses on what actually works: context engineering, agent enablement, AI-native workflows, and real-world adoption at scale.

They are especially seeking **agent and enablement-centric talks** that show how AI coding can be scaled up across dev work, teams, and platforms.

## Target Audience

Three personas they're designing for:

1. **The Pragmatic Developer** — Professional developers working hands-on with AI coding agents, looking for practical patterns from the trenches
2. **The Platform Engineer** — Engineers and technical leaders responsible for scaling agentic development best practices across teams and organisations (platforms, workflows, guardrails)
3. **The Technical Builder** — Builders creating tools, frameworks, or platforms for agentic coding, willing to open the hood, share design decisions, and demo agent-based toolchains

## Session Tracks / Categories

### 1. Context Engineering

_Designing what agents need to succeed._

- Structuring specs, rules, and skills for reliable agent behavior
- Building reusable context that works across tasks and models
- Evaluating and iterating on context as a testable artifact
- Distribution patterns: sharing context across teams and agents
- Multi-model strategies and context portability

### 2. Agent Orchestration

_Coordinating autonomy without losing control._

- Breaking down work: from backlog to agent-sized tasks
- Multi-agent patterns: parallelization, delegation, and handoffs
- Supervision and quality gates: human-in-the-loop, LLM-as-a-judge
- Bounded autonomy: scoping permissions and knowing when to interrupt
- Handling failures: retries, fallbacks, and recovery strategies

### 3. The Agent Enablement Platform

_Infrastructure that makes agents safe, observable, and affordable._

- Protocols and interoperability: MCP, A2A, and emerging standards
- Sandboxes and isolation: secure execution environments
- Observability: tracing agent actions, tool calls, and decisions
- Cost management: metering, budgets, and usage controls
- Policy enforcement: guardrails that work at platform level

### 4. Organizational Enablement

_Turning experimentation into capability._

- Rolling out agentic coding: adoption patterns that stick
- Developer education: upskilling for the context-engineering era
- Governance without gridlock: balancing autonomy and consistency
- Measuring what matters: impact metrics beyond lines of code
- Rethinking headcount: what engineering leaders need to reconsider

## Submission Requirements & Guidelines

### The Ideal Submission

- **Crisp abstracts with a clear takeaway** — no vague pitches or product demos
- **Catchy but understandable title** — no too-clever names requiring mind-reading
- **Select a primary track** that best represents your session
- **May also select a secondary track** if genuinely spanning multiple areas
- **Bold/novel perspectives welcome** — if it doesn't fit exact buckets but pushes boundaries in AI-native/agent-enabled dev, submit it
- **Avoid generic, non-coding-related AI engineer talks**
- **Real-world experiences strongly preferred** — actual use-cases, what you learned (especially when it failed)

### Important Rules

- **No sales pitches** — knowledge-sharing conference, not a sales event
- **In-person attendance required** — all sessions held in person in London; submitting confirms your availability for June 1–2
- **Travel/accommodation support available** — if needed, let them know and they'll try to help
- **Speakers get complimentary tickets**
- **You do NOT need to use Tessl** in your session (most talks won't). If you plan to use/integrate with Tessl, mention it in submission for team support.

### Diversity Commitment

They strongly encourage submissions from people of color, women, LGBTQ+ individuals, and people with disabilities.
