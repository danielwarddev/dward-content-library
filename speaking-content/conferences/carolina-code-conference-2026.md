# Carolina Code Conference 2026

## Conference Details

- **Event:** Carolina Code Conference 2026
- **Dates:** August 14-15, 2026 (Friday-Saturday)
- **Location:** Flywheel, Greenville, South Carolina
- **Website:** https://carolina.codes
- **CFP Link:** https://sessionize.com/carolina-code-conference-2026/
- **CFP Deadline:** March 31, 2026

## Speaker Benefits

- ✅ Free conference ticket
- ✅ One extra guest ticket
- ✅ Social media promotion
- ✅ Talk recorded and published
- ❌ No travel assistance
- ❌ No hotel provided

## Conference Profile

- **Focus:** Polyglot and cybersecurity conference — deliberately wide array of technologies and languages
- **Size/vibe:** ~250+ attendees, intimate community-driven event, single track
- **Talk formats:** Keynote (45-60 min), Moderate/Intermediate (20-30 min), Lightning (5-10 min)
- **Key differentiator:** Single-track format means every attendee sees every talk — high visibility but selective acceptance

## What They're Looking For

- Wide diversity of programming languages and perspectives
- Cybersecurity content is a core focus
- Community-driven, not vendor-driven
- Mix of practical and fun/creative topics
- They value unique and unconventional angles

### Key Observations

**Language diversity is defining:**

- 2025 featured: Python, Java, PHP, COBOL, Zig, F#, Pony, QuickBASIC, Elixir, Ruby, Rust, JavaScript, C#, Go
- This is intentionally polyglot — they clearly seek language variety
- C# / .NET was not heavily represented in 2025

**Tone is creative and fun:**

- Talks have personality ("Godfather's Guide," "Living on the Edge," "Having FUN")
- Mix of practical and whimsical
- Unusual/niche topics are celebrated (COBOL, QuickBASIC, Pony language)

**Single-track = high bar:**

- Only ~24-28 talks accepted total (12-14 per day)
- Every talk needs to appeal broadly since everyone sees it
- Keynotes are 45-60 min, regular talks 20-30 min — concise format

**Cybersecurity is a pillar:**

- Featured as keynote content
- Multiple security-adjacent talks each year

## Submission Recommendations

### ⭐ BEST FITS

#### 1. The Copilot Gap: What You're Missing With AI Coding Assistants

**Format:** Moderate (20-30 min)  
**Why it fits:**

- AI is clearly of interest (2025 had AI search engine keynote, AI hiring talk, reading AI's mind)
- Language-agnostic topic — works for a polyglot audience
- Practical, relatable to all developers regardless of stack
- The "gap" angle is more interesting than a generic AI talk

**Positioning tip:** Emphasize that this applies to ALL developers, not just C#/.NET devs. Frame examples using multiple languages if possible.

---

#### 2. Hearing and Being Heard - Getting the Entire Team to Speak

**Format:** Moderate (20-30 min)  
**Why it fits:**

- 2025 had career/soft skills talks ("AI: Your Hiring Manager, Career Coach")
- Single-track conferences value talks that resonate with everyone
- A team dynamics talk is universally applicable
- The intimate setting of ~250 people aligns with this talk's spirit

---

#### 3. How to Measure Developer Productivity

**Format:** Moderate (20-30 min)  
**Why it fits:**

- 2025 had "Throughput: The Most Important Metric" — shows interest in productivity/metrics
- Universal appeal across all technology stacks
- Fits the career/process angle they value

---

### 🤔 MODERATE FIT

#### 4. No More SQLite - TestContainers for EF Core

**Fit challenge:** Very .NET-specific in a polyglot conference. However, the container testing concept is universal.  
**If submitting:** Reposition as "Better Integration Testing with Containers" and mention that the concept applies to any language, with C#/.NET as the demo vehicle.

#### 5. Testing Your Tests: Mutation Testing with Stryker

**Fit challenge:** Stryker.NET is .NET-specific, but mutation testing as a concept is language-agnostic.  
**If submitting:** Lead with the mutation testing concept broadly, show Stryker as one implementation. Mention Stryker also has JS support.

#### 6. Contract Testing with Pact

**Better fit than other testing talks** because Pact is truly polyglot — supports many languages. Aligns with their diversity ethos.

### ⚠️ HARDER SELLS

- **Getting Started With AI For .NET Devs** — Too .NET-specific for this audience
- **xUnit Expanded** — Too .NET-specific
- **C# IaC on AWS with CDK** — Too narrow (C# + AWS)
- **Pulumi with C#** — Could work if positioned as language-agnostic IaC, but Pulumi supports many languages
- **GitHub Copilot SDK** — Too niche for a single-track conference

---

### 💡 SUBMISSION STRATEGY

Carolina Code is single-track with only ~25 slots total. Submit 2-3 of your most universally appealing talks:

1. **The Copilot Gap** — Broad AI topic, appeals to all
2. **Hearing and Being Heard** — Universal soft skills, great for intimate setting
3. **Measure Developer Productivity** — Universal appeal, process-focused

Consider also submitting a **lightning talk (5-10 min)** as a foot-in-the-door — they're easier to get accepted and build brand recognition.

---

### 🎯 SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

- **No travel assistance** — Factor in travel costs to Greenville, SC when deciding to submit
- **Recordings are published** — Great for building your speaking portfolio
- **Single track = high visibility** — Every attendee sees your talk, which is excellent exposure
- **20-30 minute format** — Your talks may need to be condensed from typical 60-minute versions

---

### ❓ Questions to Consider

1. Are you willing to self-fund travel to Greenville, SC? No speaker travel support.
2. Can you condense your talks into 20-30 minutes? Most of your talks are designed for 60 minutes.
3. Would you consider submitting a lightning talk (5-10 min) as a supplementary submission?
4. For The Copilot Gap, can you show examples in multiple languages to fit the polyglot audience?
