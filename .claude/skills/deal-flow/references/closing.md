# 4. The Closing Conversation

**Job:** get a decision.

If the first three conversations went well, this one is short and slightly boring. **A closing
conversation that requires persuasion is a symptom of a Value conversation that didn't happen.**

## The Proposal Is a Confirmation, Not a Pitch

A proposal restates what was already agreed. It should contain no new information and no persuasion.
If the buyer is learning something from the document, the process broke earlier.

Keep it to one page:

1. **Their situation, in their words** — pulled from Value conversation notes
2. **The outcome they said they wanted**
3. **What's included** — the 3-hour agenda, team size, remote/on-site
4. **What's not included** — explicit exclusions prevent scope creep
5. **Price and payment terms**
6. **The next step** — one action, with a date

## Offering Options

Where practical, give three options rather than one, ordered high to low. This changes the question
from *"yes or no?"* to *"which one?"* and lets the buyer self-select budget.

| Option | Shape | Rough anchor |
| ------ | ----- | ------------ |
| **A** | Workshop + follow-up session a few weeks later + team-specific custom instructions | Highest |
| **B** | The standard 3-hour workshop | $2,500–3,500 |
| **C** | Remote-only, shorter, or a single-team pilot | Lowest |

**Caveats for v1:** don't invent options that create real extra work at 2 hrs/night, and never present
options that weren't grounded in the Value conversation. If in doubt, sell B alone — a single clear
offer beats a confusing menu.

## Terms Worth Fixing Early

- **Payment:** 50% to book, 50% on delivery. Or 100% up front for a first engagement.
- **Scheduling:** a named date, not "sometime next quarter." Undated deals decay.
- **Cap:** ~15 attendees. State it.
- **Cancellation/reschedule:** a simple stated policy protects limited evening time.
- **Invoicing through the LLC**, never personally.

## Common Objections

| Objection | What it usually means | Response shape |
| --------- | --------------------- | -------------- |
| "That's more than we expected" | Value conversation was thin | Return to outcome and cost of inaction — don't discount reflexively |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | Testing, or genuine budget limit | Reduce scope, not price. Option C exists for this. |
| "Let me check with my manager" | Wrong person, or normal process | "Happy to join that conversation — what would help them decide?" |
| "Can you customize it for our codebase?" | Real interest | Not in v1. Name it as a follow-on engagement. |
| "We'll circle back next quarter" | Usually no | "Should I follow up on a specific date, or is this a not-right-now?" |

**Never discount to close a first deal.** The price signals seriousness, and a discounted first client
anchors every future negotiation. Cut scope instead.

## After a Yes

- Confirm date, headcount, location, and prerequisites in writing the same day.
- Invoice immediately.
- Ask for a testimonial after delivery — this is the missing credibility asset, and the moment right
  after a successful workshop is the only easy time to ask.
- Ask the referral question: "Who else here, or at companies you know, has the same problem?"

## After a No

- Ask what tipped it. The answer is free market research.
- Log it in the deal log with the reason.
- Stay in the Probative relationship. A no today is often a yes after the next reorg or license renewal.
