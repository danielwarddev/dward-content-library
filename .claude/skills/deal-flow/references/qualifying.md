# 2. The Qualifying Conversation

**Job:** decide whether a real opportunity exists — and whether you want it.

This is a **mutual** filter, not a pitch. The goal is a fast yes or a fast no. At 2 hrs/night, the
most expensive outcome is a slow maybe.

## What Must Be True

Four things. Missing any one is grounds to stop or park.

| Test | Question behind it | Red flag |
| ---- | ------------------ | -------- |
| **Real problem** | Is something actually wrong, and do they feel it? | "We're interested in AI stuff generally" |
| **Desire to solve** | Are they motivated to act *now*? | No trigger event; vague timeline |
| **Authority / access** | Can this person approve, or introduce whoever can? | Enthusiastic person with no budget line |
| **Fit** | Is this the workshop, and are you the right person? | They want a custom 6-month engagement |

## The Trigger Event

The strongest qualifier for this offer: **did they buy Copilot licenses in the last 6–12 months, and
can they tell whether it's working?**

That's a specific, funded, painful moment. Without it, there is usually no urgency and no budget.

## Questions Worth Asking

Ask; don't present. Order roughly as listed.

- "Did your team end up rolling out Copilot licenses? How long ago?"
- "How's it going — are people actually using it, or has it fizzled?"
- "What made you look at it in the first place?"
- "What happens if this stays the way it is for another six months?"
- "Who besides you would care about fixing that?"
- "Have you tried anything already? How'd that go?"
- "If you did do something about it, who decides — and how does that usually work there?"

**Note what's absent:** any description of the workshop. That comes later, and only if this passes.

## Employer Conflict Check

Before proceeding on any opportunity, confirm:

- Not a current client of the employer
- No conflict with the approved side-work conditions
- Public marketing sign-off in place (Goal 0)

If uncertain, stop and check. This is not a judgment call worth getting wrong.

## Disqualifying Well

Disqualifying is a feature. Done cleanly it *raises* authority and often reopens later.

- Be direct and warm: "Honestly, I don't think a workshop is what you need right now."
- Say why, and say what would change it: "If licenses go out next quarter, come back to me."
- Offer a genuinely useful pointer, then stop. Don't slide into free consulting to soften the no.

**Disqualify when:** no trigger event, no authority and no path to it, they want a custom build, the
timeline is "sometime," or the fit is with the employer rather than you.

## Coaching Notes

- **The failure mode for this seller:** being helpful. A qualifying call can turn into a free
  mini-consultation in about four minutes. Teaching is the product — don't give the diagnosis away.
- **A referral ask is a qualifying question.** "Who decides on training?" tests authority without
  pitching. It's the lowest-friction entry to this conversation.
- **Don't send a proposal from here.** It's far too early; a proposal now is a price with nothing to
  anchor to.

## Exit Criteria

Advance to the Value conversation when there's a real problem, felt urgency, and either authority or a
warm introduction to it. Get the next conversation scheduled before this one ends.
