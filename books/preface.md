This book will be opinionated. I believe that in order to talk in a meaninful way about usage patterns, what to do, and what not to do with AI, it must be opinionated. Otherwise, this book would just be a list of GitHub Copilot features.

This is partly because there is very little scientific data around what consistently works or doesn't work when working with AI. Even if there were, it would probably be situational, depending on certain models, tools, and the like. There also probably never will be much scientific data, if any, because that is very expensive to obtain for not much return to whoever is conducting it. Plus, as with any technology, AI will get better over time, requiring newer studies.

Another contributor is that, much like with normal programming and design patterns, choosing when to use which prompt technique is often less science and more art or trial-and-error. 

As a consequence, the direction I give in this book will be the results of things I have perceived as working well either for myself or others. Thus, you should keep an open mind about different techniques you see with AI, but also be cautious.
