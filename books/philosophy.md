When working with AI, it's worthwhile to know how our brains interact with it, and with problem solving in general. You may have heard of studies showing things like AI usage being correlated with reduced critical thinking, less secure code, and lower memory retention.

I believe all of those things are true, but I also believe that AI is a good tool when used correctly. We know about technical debt, but when you allow AI to perform the work for you, you take on "cognitive debt." That is, you get the result without the understanding it took to get there, and you probably need to understand it in order to successfully work on that same project for long periods of time.

I think there are two solutions to this. The first is to think through the problem yourself first before asking AI, then using it to fact check yourself. The second is get an answer from AI, but ask it to explain it more or go to official sources (like docs) for explanation.

The core lesson is this: using AI in new domains does not replace the learning of those domains. In that learning, the bottleneck is still our brain and how it processes information (and, as far as I can tell, it always will be).
