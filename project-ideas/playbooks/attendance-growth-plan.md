# Growing Attendance for the San Antonio / Austin .NET User Group (Remote)

**Generated:** 2026-08-20
**Context:** How to increase live attendance, RSVPs, reach, and regulars for a remote-only monthly user group currently promoted via LinkedIn, X, Bluesky, and Meetup email (1 week out + 1 day out).

---

## Where you actually are

| Group | Members | Typical live attendance | Show rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| San Antonio | 220 | ~5 | ~2.3% |
| Austin | 840 | ~10-15 | ~1.5% |
| **Combined** | **~1,060** | **~15-20** | **~1.7%** |

For a free remote monthly meetup, 1.5-2% of total membership showing up live is roughly normal. That's the bad news and the good news: you're not broken, you're just at the default. Getting to 3-4% is very achievable and would double you.

### Diagnose before you optimize

The single most useful number you don't have yet: **RSVP → attend conversion.**

- If you get ~40 RSVPs and 15 show → **50% show rate, which is normal.** Your bottleneck is *awareness/interest* (not enough RSVPs). Focus on Tier 2 (other people's audiences).
- If you get ~70 RSVPs and 15 show → **20% show rate, which is bad.** Your bottleneck is *friction and forgetting*. Focus on Tier 1 (calendar, reminders, one-click join).

Track this for the next 3 events before deciding where to spend effort. Free events reliably see 30-50% no-shows; below 30% means something structural is wrong.

## Tier 1: Minimal effort (< 1 hour/month, mostly templates and setup-once)

These are the highest ROI-per-minute items. Do all of them.

### 1. Get the event onto their work calendar

This is the number one lever for a **noon** remote event. A meetup that isn't on someone's Outlook/Google calendar loses to whatever meeting gets booked over it. Meetup's "add to calendar" is buried and most people never tap it.

- Put a direct **"Add to your calendar"** link at the very top of the Meetup description and in both reminder emails.
- Include an `.ics` file or a Google Calendar template link in the emails.
- Explicitly say: *"Block it now, it's noon and your calendar will fill up."*

**Setup once, reuse forever.** Probably the single biggest win on this list.

### 2. Add a day-of reminder (and a 15-minutes-before nudge)

Your current cadence is week-before + day-before. Both are good, keep them. The missing one is the day itself.

| When | Channel | Message |
|---|---|---|
| 1 week out | Meetup email | Full details (you do this) |
| 1 day out | Meetup email | Reminder + calendar link (you do this) |
| **Morning of, ~8-9am** | **Meetup announcement + all 3 socials** | **"Today at noon. Here's the Zoom link."** |
| **~15 min before** | **LinkedIn + X/Bluesky reply to the morning post** | **"Starting in 15. Join us: [link]"** |

For a lunch-hour event, the "today" and "right now" reminders convert more than anything sent days earlier. Your existing [08-27-2026.md](user-group/social-posts/08-27-2026.md) schedule already has "morning of" — add the 15-minute one.

### 3. Put the Zoom link everywhere, above the fold

Every extra click between "I want to attend" and "I'm in the call" costs you people. Zoom link should be:

- The first line of the Meetup event description (not buried at the bottom)
- In every reminder email
- In the day-of social posts

Someone deciding at 11:58 will not go hunting through Meetup for it.

### 4. Create a LinkedIn Event for every meetup

This is free and badly underused for dev audiences.

- LinkedIn sends its own reminders to registrants — a second reminder system you don't maintain.
- When someone registers, **it shows up in their network's feed.** That's organic reach beyond your followers, which is the thing you can't buy.
- You can message all registrants directly from the event.

Your audience is professional developers. LinkedIn is your strongest channel and you're using maybe 40% of it.

### 5. Post the event 3-4 weeks out with a custom image

Meetup's own distribution is a channel you're leaving on the table:

- Events posted further out get included in **more weekly Meetup digest emails** and appear in the "events near you" feed longer.
- A listing with a **custom graphic** gets noticeably more clicks in the feed than the default placeholder.
- Fill out the description fully — Meetup surfaces events by topic/keyword matching.

Make one reusable branded template graphic; swap the talk title each month.

### 6. Write the Meetup title for skimmers, not for cleverness

Keep the clever title for the talk itself. In the **Meetup listing and social posts**, front-load the payoff.

- Talk title: *Don't Make Me Repeat Myself: Using Analyzers in .NET For Quality By Default*
- Listing title: *Stop Repeating Code Review Comments: Enforce Your .NET Standards in the Build*

The listing title is what a stranger scrolling Meetup sees. Make the benefit obvious in the first five words. (Your teaser posts already do this well — the "Tired of leaving the same code review comment over and over?" line is exactly right. Use that energy in the title.)

### 7. Hand every speaker a pre-written promo post

You already write excellent social copy. Write **one more version, in first person, and DM it to the speaker** with the graphic attached.

Speakers almost always want to promote but don't want to write the post. Removing that friction means their whole network sees it. This is your cheapest access to audiences you don't own.

Add to your checklist: *"Send speaker their copy-paste promo pack 2 weeks out."*

### 8. Cross-list every event to both Meetup groups

If San Antonio (220) and Austin (840) are separate groups for the same remote event, make sure **every event appears in both**, every time. 220 members who only see half your events is dead weight. Since the event is remote, there's no reason for either group to miss one.

### 9. Always announce next month's topic before you sign off

At the end of every call, and in the recap post/email: *"Same time next month — here's the topic."*

This is how you convert one-time attendees into regulars. It costs 20 seconds and compounds.

### 10. Treat the recording as a promo asset, not an archive

- Publish it within a few days, every time.
- Cut **one 60-90 second highlight clip** and post it natively to LinkedIn (native video, not a YouTube link — LinkedIn suppresses external links).
- The CTA on the clip is not "watch the recording," it's **"next month's topic + signup link."**

Recording views are top-of-funnel for future live attendance, and they're the answer to "I couldn't make noon." It also makes speaking at your group more attractive to good speakers.

## Tier 2: Moderate effort (a few hours/month)

Tier 1 fixes your conversion. Tier 2 is how you actually grow the pool. **The core principle: your ceiling on LinkedIn/X/Bluesky is your own follower count. Growth comes from borrowing other people's audiences.**

### 11. Book speakers who bring an audience

Speaker selection is a growth strategy, not just a programming decision. A guest with 10k followers who posts once will out-perform your entire month of promotion.

What you're offering them (it's genuinely valuable, lead with it):

- A low-stakes remote venue to **test-drive a conference talk** before NDC/KCDC/etc.
- A recording they can attach to future CFP submissions
- Zero travel

You already run in the conference-speaker world — that's a warm list. Aim for one "audience-bringing" guest per quarter.

### 12. Trade announcements with other remote-friendly user groups

Find 4-6 other .NET / Azure / dev groups that run remote or hybrid. Offer a straight swap: they post your event to their members, you post theirs to yours. Once a month, ~15 minutes.

Zero cost, and it's the most precisely targeted audience that exists — people who have already proven they attend user groups.

Start with: other regional .NET user groups, Azure/Cloud groups, and Texas-area dev groups. A shared "remote .NET user group" calendar/consortium with 3-4 other organizers is even better if anyone bites.

### 13. Seed employer Slack channels through your regulars

Ask 3-5 of your reliable attendees to drop the event in their company's `#dotnet` or `#engineering` Slack.

**One internal Slack post at a 500-developer company beats a month of LinkedIn posts.** People trust a coworker's recommendation and the event lands in a channel they already read. This is the highest-leverage thing on the entire list and it takes one DM.

### 14. Use sponsors and recruiters as distribution, not as funders

You said no bribes — agreed, and you don't need them. What you want from a sponsor is **reach**, not money.

The deal: they get a 2-3 minute intro at the top of the call. In exchange, they promote the event to their own list.

- **Recruiters** have large local developer networks and it is literally their job to talk to devs. Their promotion is the payment.
- **Vendors** can supply giveaway items (see next item) and post to their community channels.

### 15. Do the giveaway right: raffle at the end, present-only

Free books/licenses aren't bribes, they're a reason to **stay through the Q&A** instead of dropping off at minute 35.

Mechanics that work:
- Announce the giveaway **at the start**, draw it **at the very end**, and you must be **on the call** to win.
- Ask the winner to unmute and say hi — that's how lurkers become regulars.

Where to get items for free (these programs exist specifically for user groups):
- **JetBrains** — free license giveaways for user groups
- **Manning / Pragmatic Bookshelf / Packt / Apress** — ebook codes for meetups
- **Telerik/Progress, DevExpress** — license giveaways
- Conference organizers you're connected with — spare tickets

### 16. Turn each talk into a written artifact

A short post or blog writeup: the one key takeaway, a code snippet, and a link to the recording. Cross-post to your blog.

This is evergreen inbound — it keeps pulling in people who search the topic months later, and every one of them lands on a page that mentions the group. It also double-dips with your existing blog content pipeline.

### 17. Ask for exactly one referral, out loud, once

At the end of the call: *"If you got something out of this, send the link to one coworker before next month."*

Direct asks work. Most organizers never make them.

### 18. Post where local devs actually hang out

- Austin/San Antonio **tech Slacks and Discords** (these exist and are active)
- Local subreddits' tech/jobs threads — r/Austin, r/sanantonio
- **Skip r/dotnet** for direct promotion; it's hostile to self-promo and you'll spend goodwill for nothing

## Things I'd be skeptical of

| Idea | Why to be cautious |
|---|---|
| Moving off the noon slot | Noon is genuinely good for remote — it's the one hour people can justify to their employer. Evening competes with family time and in-person groups. Don't chase this. |
| Paid ads | Free meetups don't convert well on paid social, and CAC is meaningless when the "purchase" is a free Zoom link. |
| Posting more often on your existing channels | You're already posting well (5 posts per event is solid). Diminishing returns — the same followers see it. Growth is in *other people's* audiences, not more frequency in yours. |
| Chasing raw member count | 1,060 members producing 15 attendees means member count isn't your constraint. Activating existing members beats recruiting new ones. |
| Making the recording the headline | If people know the recording is guaranteed, some will always choose it over live. Publish it, but frame live as where Q&A, giveaways, and conversation happen. |

## What to measure

Track these in a simple table per event:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| RSVPs (SA + Austin, separately) | Top of funnel |
| Live peak attendance | The actual goal |
| **RSVP → attend %** | Tells you whether to focus Tier 1 or Tier 2 |
| Returning attendees vs. first-timers | Are you building regulars or churning? |
| Recording views at 7 / 30 days | Total reach |
| Referral source (ask in chat: "how'd you hear about this?") | Tells you which channel actually works |

That last one — a single question in the Zoom chat at the start — will teach you more in three months than any analytics dashboard.

## Suggested rollout

### Month 1 — instrument and remove friction (Tier 1)
- Add calendar link / `.ics` to Meetup description and both emails
- Add morning-of and 15-min-before reminders
- Move Zoom link to the top of the event description
- Start tracking RSVP → attend and asking "how'd you hear about us?" in chat
- Add speaker promo pack to your prep checklist

### Month 2 — expand owned distribution (Tier 1)
- Create a LinkedIn Event for the meetup
- Build the reusable event graphic template
- Post the event 4 weeks out to both groups
- Publish recording + one highlight clip, CTA to next month
- Announce next month's topic on the call

### Month 3 — borrow other audiences (Tier 2)
- Reach out to 5 other user groups for announcement swaps
- DM 5 regulars asking them to post in their work Slack
- Line up one audience-bringing guest speaker for the following quarter
- Approach 1-2 recruiters/vendors for a 2-minute intro in exchange for promotion
- Line up a giveaway (JetBrains or a publisher) and run the present-only raffle

## Notes

- **If I had to pick three:** (1) calendar link + day-of reminders, (2) regulars posting in their employer Slack channels, (3) speakers with audiences who promote their own talk. Those three are where most of the upside is.
- Your existing social post templates in [user-group/social-posts](user-group/social-posts) are already strong — the teaser series with distinct angles is better than most groups do. The gap isn't copy quality, it's **distribution reach and calendar friction**.
- Expect a lag. Attendance growth from Tier 2 shows up 2-3 months after you start, not the next event. Don't kill a tactic after one month.
