fund and aim the building work, which is the part that's actually most enjoyable.

## Diagnosis: Why Nothing Has Sold Yet

1. **Nobody has ever been asked.** `blog/work-with-me-draft.md` is still a draft — no live page, no price,
   no named list of people to send it to. A thing that has never been offered can't have failed to sell.
2. **The free talks have no capture mechanism.** ~25 presentations delivered (see `blog/presentation-posts/`).
   Each ends with applause and no CTA, no email capture, no follow-up. The expensive part of selling — an hour
   of demonstrated expertise to a target-rich room — is already being done, then dropped.
3. **The offer is too big to be a first yes.** The 6-week program (3–5 hrs/week) requires budget approval,
   manager sign-off, and scheduling across a team. Nobody buys that from someone they haven't bought from before.
4. **Rapport is not the problem.** The rapport exists at scale. It has simply never been converted.

## Decisions Made

### The Offer

Use the existing, already-delivered **3-hour "Zero to Hero with GitHub Copilot"** workshop
(`speaking-content/workshops/github-copilot/copilot-workshop-3hr-plan.md`) as the on-ramp.

- One team, up to ~15 developers
- 3 hours, remote or on-site
- Fixed price, **~$2,500–3,500**
- No discovery call, no proposal, no customization in v1

**Why this shape:** the goal is to stay under a single engineering manager's discretionary budget so one
person can approve it over email. Free-to-$500 attracts tire-kickers and signals amateur. The 6-week program
becomes the natural upsell — sold to people who have already bought once.

**The math:** 4 sales/year = $12k. Selling a packaged outcome rather than hours matters here — hourly coaching
requires finding recurring hours every month forever, which does not fit 2 hrs/night.

### The Web Presence

- **`danielward.dev`** — the offer. One page: outcome, agenda, price, one booking button. Lives under the LLC,
  reads as a business to a manager forwarding a proposal, and creates useful daylight from the personal brand.
- **`daninacan.com`** — stays the content engine. All SEO and traffic already live here. Every CTA points to
  the one page.
- **Resist building a real site.** One page is enough.

### The Sales Motion

**One-to-one, not broadcast.** The distinction is the addressing, not the medium — DM, email, text, or hallway
conversation all work. A post addressed to everyone converts near zero even with a good audience.

- **Target the trigger event:** companies that bought Copilot licenses in the last 6–12 months and can't tell
  if they're working. That's a specific, painful, budgeted moment. Everyone else is a no.
- **The list already exists**, just unwritten: user group attendees, workshop attendees, blog subscribers,
  MVP network. Write down 30–40 names where the company plausibly rolled out Copilot recently.
- **Lead with a referral ask, not a pitch:** *"Did y'all end up rolling out Copilot licenses? I'm putting
  together a half-day workshop for teams and trying to figure out who actually decides on that kind of thing."*
  This asks for a name, not a sale — nearly everyone answers, and it routes to the real buyer without cold outreach.
- **Cadence:** after each user group, send ~8 individual follow-ups over the following week. Eight of those
  beats a hundred impressions on a post.
- **Why this works here and not for a stranger:** these people already know you're credible. You've taught them.

### Higher-Leverage Channel (identified, unexplored)

Microsoft and GitHub account teams, and Copilot resellers/partners. They sell licenses, get asked "can someone
train our devs?", and have no one to point to. An MVP with a proven workshop is exactly who they want in their
back pocket. A handful of these relationships could outproduce all direct outreach.

## Open Questions

1. **Employer clarity on public marketing.** Existing approval covers side work (must not interfere with the
   job, must not involve a current client) but not *public marketing of a similar offering*. Posting doesn't
   violate either condition, but it changes visibility: a current client could approach unprompted, and the
   employer's sales team may perceive overlap. Ask specifically about public marketing and get it in writing.
   Also worth asking whether they'd route or co-deliver work instead — some consultancies value an MVP who
   teaches publicly. *(Not legal advice — the written version is what protects you.)*
2. **Build the named list** — 30–40 people/companies. Not started.
3. **Draft the referral message**, the closing-slide CTA, and the post-talk follow-up sequence.
4. **Write the landing page copy** for the half-day offer.
5. **Explore the partner channel** — Microsoft/GitHub account teams and Copilot resellers.
6. **What to actually build** — deferred. Still the most enjoyable work, and consulting income is what funds
   and aims it.

# SMART Goals

**Added:** August 31, 2026

"Make $1k/mo on the side" is a direction, not a goal — it has no first action and no way to tell whether
today went well. Below it is broken into one goal you can finish tonight, then the sequence that follows.

## 30-Minute Rule: The One Thing to Do First

The overall goal is far too large for 30 minutes. Extracted sub-task: **send the employer email about public
marketing.** This is the correct first move because it gates everything public — the landing page, the
LinkedIn post, and any outreach that names the offer. It also costs almost nothing and removes the specific
ambiguity that already produced a gut-check.

> **SMART Goal 0 — Unblock the public side**
>
> Send one email to my manager by **end of day Wednesday, September 2, 2026** asking for explicit written
> confirmation that I can publicly market a half-day GitHub Copilot workshop under my LLC, and whether the
> company would prefer to route or co-deliver that work instead — so that every downstream step
> (landing page, outreach, posting) can proceed without ambiguity.
>
> - **Measure:** Email sent; written reply received and saved.
> - **Deadline:** Sent by Sep 2, 2026. Follow up if no reply by Sep 9.

> **SMART Goal 0b — Get into the Microsoft field orbit**
>
> Email my Microsoft contact by **Friday, September 4, 2026** to volunteer as host for the next available
> Microsoft-sponsored developer event (Copilot Dev Days or equivalent), and commit to attending with the
> explicit objective of leaving with the names and contact details of at least 2 Microsoft field/account
> team members — building the referral channel that removes the need to source buyers myself.

> - **Measure:** Email sent; event date on calendar; ≥2 named field contacts captured afterward.
> - **Cost:** Free to run, Microsoft-funded, ~20 minutes to initiate.
> - **Rule:** Do not sell from the stage. The relationship outlives any single deal.

## Microsoft-Sponsored Events (Copilot Dev Days)

Optionally-hosted, Microsoft-funded events. This is the strongest single item in the inventory because it
delivers four things simultaneously:

1. A room of developers at companies **actively adopting Copilot** — the exact trigger event.
2. Positioning as host/instructor rather than vendor.
3. Microsoft-funded, so no cost to run.
4. **A working relationship with the Microsoft field people who organize it** — the same people who field
   "can someone train our devs?" requests.

That fourth point is the real prize. It converts the outreach problem from "find buyers" into "be the person
the account team points to."

**Cautions:**
- Do not sell from the stage. The relationship is worth more than any single deal.
- Check the program terms on commercial use of sponsored events.
- The play: run it well, meet the field team, let the workshop surface in conversation afterward.

## Online Courses: Assessment

**Verdict: not now. Revisit after the workshop has run several times and an email list exists.**

| Platform | Read |
| -------- | ---- |
| **Udemy** | Saturated and price-collapsed. Perpetual $10–20 sales, minority revenue share on platform-sourced students. A volume business requiring an audience that doesn't exist yet. |
| **LinkedIn Learning** | Audition/invite-only. MVP status genuinely helps entry. Flat production fee plus modest royalties, long lead times, no control over pricing or promotion. |
| **Pluralsight** | Audition-based, historically better royalties, but the company has had real financial turbulence. |
| **O'Reilly (live)** | The standout — but it's live training, not a recorded course. See Tier 3. |

**Why not in parallel:**

- A recorded course is 80–150 hours of production — roughly the entire year at 2 hrs/night, spent before
  earning anything.
- It has the *identical* failure mode as the workshop: no audience to sell it to. Solving the harder problem
  second, again.
- **Subject-matter decay is the decisive argument.** Copilot ships meaningful changes quarterly. A course
  recorded today is visibly stale in six months and dead in twelve, meaning permanent re-recording. Live
  delivery is *advantaged* by the same churn — being current is the product.

**The version worth doing later:** once the workshop has run a few times and a list exists, record the
workshop already being delivered and sell it directly. Near-zero marginal content cost, full margin, and an
audience that exists.
