## The Economics Are As Bad As Described

Traditional royalties on a ~$40 technical book run roughly $1–2/copy, and a typical technical title sells a
few thousand copies lifetime. That's low four figures for **300–600 hours** of work.

**As direct income, this is the worst option discussed anywhere in this folder.** It is not a path to $1k/mo.

## "Powerful Business Card" — Scrutinized

The phrase is doing a lot of work. What a book genuinely buys:

- **Durable, transferable authority.** "Author of *X*" outlives any single talk and reaches people who have
  never met you.
- **Gatekeeper access.** Conference committees, podcast bookers, O'Reilly, and corporate training buyers all
  treat "author" as a filter. That is Tier 3 in the channel inventory — the people who sell for you.
- **A closing asset.** Handing a manager a book you wrote makes a $3k workshop feel obviously cheap.

### The catch: most of that is already in hand

**Microsoft MVP is the same credential**, obtained without 500 hours. Talks already get accepted at NDC,
KCDC, and GitHub Universe. The *marginal* authority a book adds to this profile is far smaller than it would
be for an unknown consultant — which is generally who that advice comes from.

The advice is sound. It's aimed at someone who doesn't already have the credential.

## Topic Determines Everything

| | Copilot book | .NET testing book |
| - | ------------ | ----------------- |
| Shelf life | ~1 year | 3–5 years |
| Existing shelf | 4+ books already published (Packt, O'Reilly, MS Press, Apress), several with 2–7 reviews | Thinner, and no canonical modern title |
| Source material | Extensive (see `books/2026-03-18-copilot-book-outline.md`) | 3 years of posts + testingwithdot.net |
| Points at which offer? | Copilot workshop | **Testing coaching** |
| Compounds or evaporates? | Evaporates | Compounds |

A Copilot book means spending ~500 hours on a business card with an expiration date, on a crowded and
weakly-performing shelf. **A .NET testing book is a genuinely different proposition** — same reversal as the
course argument in [building-plan.md](building-plan.md), where topic decay decided the outcome.

## Recommendation: Not Now — Earn the Right First

**Sequence it behind evidence:**

1. Ship testingwithdot.net (Goal B1 in [building-plan.md](building-plan.md)).
2. Build the email list (Goal B2).
3. Sell testing coaching at least once.
4. **Then** decide — with validated material, a proven audience, and a list to launch to.

If the site draws real traffic and the coaching sells, the book largely writes itself from material that has
already been tested on readers. If the site flops, ~500 hours have been saved.

## If It Ever Happens: Publisher, Not Self-Published

Counterintuitive but consistent with the reasoning above: **pitch a publisher** (Manning, Pragmatic
Bookshelf, Apress, O'Reilly). Self-publishing pays meaningfully better per copy and delivers *less* of the
only thing actually being purchased — distribution and the external credential.

Since royalties are not the point, optimize for the imprint on the cover.

Submission paths are already researched in `blog/brainstorms/2026-03-12-copilot-book-market-research.md`.

## Notes

- **The real cost is opportunity cost.** 500 hours at 2 hrs/night is roughly eight months — the entire
  first-year plan, spent on the lowest-paying option.
- **Watch for the trap:** writing a book is a comfortable, legible-feeling activity that postpones the
  uncomfortable one (asking people to buy the workshop). Book-writing is a very sophisticated form of
  procrastination.
- Revisit this document after Goal B1 and the first coaching sale — not before.
